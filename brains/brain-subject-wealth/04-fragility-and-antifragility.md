# Fragility and antifragility

## Position

A fragile asset gains value in calm and breaks in disorder. An antifragile asset gains with volatility. The split is measurable: plot excess return against volatility and the slopes run opposite.

The choice between them is a bet on which part of the distribution the next decade sits in. Fragile assets pay in the 80% that is normal. Antifragile assets pay in the tails, and cost you while nothing happens. Holding the wrong one is not a small error, because systems reconfigure in short violent episodes rather than gradually.

## Evidence

- 2026, Richard Detente: gold rises as confidence falls. The yen rises in crisis because Japanese savers repatriate capital, which makes it antifragile by social structure rather than by innovation.
- 2026, Richard Detente: Dubai and Swiss property rise with European political volatility, because wealth relocates and families will not move far. Swiss property rises until Europe runs out of rich people.
- 2026, Richard Detente: Bitcoin is antifragile in theory, since it answers a crisis of monetary confidence. It has too few observations to confirm it in the measurements yet.
- 2026, Richard Detente: an antifragile asset in calm conditions is a vaccine with no epidemic. Paper money invented while gold coins work has no buyers.
- 2026, Richard Detente: snow accumulates as flakes for months, and 30 seconds of avalanche reconfigures the slope. Revolutions, wars and monetary collapses occupy very little time and do all the transformation.

## Data

- Normal conditions occupy about 80% of a distribution, tails share the rest
- Antifragile assets named: gold, sugar, yen, Dubai property, Swiss property, Bitcoin
- Measurement: excess return plotted against volatility, opposite slopes
