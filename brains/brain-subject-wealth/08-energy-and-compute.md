# Energy and compute

## Position

AI's constraint is not how much electricity it needs, it is how much lands on one point of the grid. A grid behaves like a web: spread the load and it carries a lot, concentrate it and it tears. One gigawatt planted in one place deforms everything around it.

That makes flexible demand valuable rather than merely tolerated. A load that can stop on request raises the grid's load factor when power is cheap and gets paid to disappear when it is scarce. Since a grid is a fixed-cost business, the most expensive kilowatt is the one nobody consumes, because idle capacity still has to be amortised across everything else.

Hyperscale data centres cannot offer that flexibility. A three-week training run across 300,000 chips cannot pause, so they demand guaranteed supply, which forces peak capacity upward. Building gas generation takes five years and permits take ten, against AI timelines of months.

## Evidence

- 2026, Richard Detente: his bet from 2022 was that AI would exhaust concentrated grid capacity and be forced to distribute, and that no distributed AI network existed yet only meant one had to appear.
- 2026, Richard Detente: Bitcoin mining was the entry, because it starts and stops instantly. Mining farms in Texas raised the load factor, which lowered prices locally, and got paid to shut off during a polar storm when the megawatt hour went from 40 dollars to 500.
- 2026, Richard Detente: Data Factory's 2023 fundraising pitched electrical infrastructure rather than mining, despite mining being 100% of revenue, because the thesis was seizing megawatts where nobody wanted them until high performance compute arrived.
- 2026, Richard Detente: Data Factory merged with Hive and Polycloud into Antimatter, which now runs container-format distributed data centres on the same slabs the mining farms used. Twenty researchers at Inria work on converting centralised data centre algorithms for distributed infrastructure.
- 2026, Richard Detente: buying CoreWeave at a price to book of 11 means 11 dollars buys 1 dollar of compute capacity, the rest being expected growth. His answer is to sell shares in owned Texas assets at book value instead.

## Data

- AI projected at 3% of world electricity by 2030
- Texas power: 40 to 45 dollars a megawatt hour normally, 500 in a polar storm
- Nuclear plant: 10 billion to build, needs about 1 billion a year regardless of output
- Gas generation: 5 years to build, 10 years of permits
- Training runs: 200,000 to 300,000 chips synchronised over 3 weeks
- CoreWeave price to book: 11
- Antimatter revenue approaching 1 billion in 2026
- Polycloud format: 20 and 40 foot shipping containers
- Inria team: 20 researchers
