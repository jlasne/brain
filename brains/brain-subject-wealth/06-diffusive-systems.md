# Diffusive systems

## Position

A diffusive system takes energy in, fights to keep its own structure, and emits heat plus information. Since the big bang these systems have grown steadily denser in energy dissipated per unit of mass, and the curve is exponential.

Two consequences carry into investing. Innovation is structural rather than accidental, so value will keep moving. And the cycles are shortening, which is log-periodicity, so the interval between displacements keeps compressing.

The denser a system gets, the smaller the space it occupies. Most of the universe is cold and empty while complexity concentrates in tiny pockets. Human societies repeat it: the Sahara empty, Tokyo dense.

## Evidence

- 2026, Richard Detente, crediting Charles Gave and Didier Darcet: economics is treated as a hard science, modelled the way physics models systems, with direct parallels for mass and fragility.
- 2026, Richard Detente: the universe starts hot, dense and uniform, then expansion cools it, and cooling produces particles, atoms, molecules, life. Macro structures generate micro structures which generate macro structures.
- 2026, Richard Detente: human societies now dissipate more energy per unit of mass than galaxies, stars or particles, and they sit at the top of an exponential curve running since the big bang.

## Data

- Energy dissipated per unit mass: exponential since the big bang
- Human societies rank above galaxies, stars and particles on that measure
- Innovation cycles: about 10 years now, against generations before the industrial revolution
- The concept of progress became perceptible within one lifetime roughly two centuries ago
