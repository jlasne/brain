# Monetary debasement

## Position

The dollar prints about 8% a year. Measured in dollars, any portfolio returning less than 8% is losing purchasing power while appearing to hold. No conventionally prudent allocation clears 8%, so prudence and debasement together guarantee erosion.

Debasement is a tax on holders of future money. A government that will not raise taxes and will not cut promises taxes generations not yet born, who cannot object.

## Evidence

- 2026, Richard Detente: at 8% annual dollar printing, a portfolio under 8% is being sheared. Nothing marketed as prudent returns more than 8%, which is why the arithmetic is rarely stated publicly.
- 2026, Richard Detente: the three options for spending beyond revenue are taxes that provoke resistance, debt which taxes the unborn, and printing which erodes existing wealth without a visible act.
- 2026, Richard Detente: Switzerland runs balanced accounts, France and the United States do not, and China runs internal debt which settles the question with its own population instead.

## Data

- Dollar monetary printing: about 8% a year
- No prudent instrument returns above 8%
- Balanced accounts cited: Switzerland. Unbalanced: France, United States
