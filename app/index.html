Read it once. Keep the reasoning.

Most of what you read leaves nothing behind. Octopus keeps the thinking. Every source becomes a view you hold, and it tells you when something new disagrees with it.

A brain is one subject, and one sentence decides what belongs in it.

Write that sentence and the brain has a gate. Everything you feed it is measured against the line, so it can grow for years and still answer as one mind. "Health" names a folder. "Sleep, recovery and training load for my own routine" names a brain.

Three things happen, and you are in the middle of one.

01

You feed it

A video, an article, a study. It reads the whole thing once and keeps what carries a number, a date or a reason. The text itself is thrown away.

02

It argues

Before anything is kept, it shows you what is new, what you already knew, and what contradicts a view you hold. You say which one wins.

03

You ask

Ask in plain words. The answer carries the numbers inside it, and the people and dates it rests on sit underneath.

What keeps it honest.

  • A view you replace keeps its date and moves into the evidence. Nothing is deleted.
  • Each view is rewritten from everything standing behind it, so it reads as one position rather than a pile of quotes.
  • Disagreements stay visible, both sides dated, instead of being smoothed over.
  • The same source never lands twice, and a claim you already hold is named as a repeat.

Your brains, read by any assistant you use.

A brain you mark public can be read from your own Claude, so the assistant answers from what you have gathered rather than from the open internet. A brain you keep private stays with you.

Open source, and built in the open.

All of it is public: the rules the brains follow, the app, the connector. Read it, fork it, run it against your own database, or bring a brain of your own to the shared ones. Octopus is an entry in The Build Games, a September event judging projects on how well they stand in for paid software.
