Six months on, you keep the conclusion and lose every reason for it.

You saved the link. You highlighted the paragraph. You wrote the note. What none of that gives back is why you believed it, who said it, on what date, and whether something newer has since contradicted it.

So the same question gets researched twice. A view you formed in March survives into November because nothing ever checked it. Two sources disagree in your library and you never find out.

A brain holds a position, not a copy of the page.

Feed it a source. It reads the whole thing once, keeps what carries a number, a date or a reason, and throws the text away. What stays is a view, plus the evidence standing behind that view.

When the next source arrives, the view gets rewritten from everything behind it, this one included. It never becomes a longer list of quotes.

One sentence decides what belongs in a brain.

You write that sentence when you create it. Everything you feed the brain is measured against the line, so the brain can grow for years and still answer as one mind.

"Health" names a folder. "Sleep, recovery and training load for my own routine" names a brain. The second one can reject things, which is what makes it useful.

Three things happen, and you decide one of them.

01

You feed it

A video, an article, a study. Read once, in passes, then discarded. The link and the author stay so the claim can be traced back.

02

It argues

One card shows what is new, what you already hold, and every claim that contradicts a view. You say which side wins, or keep both.

03

You ask

Ask in plain words. The answer carries the numbers, and names the authors and dates it rests on underneath.

What that buys you.

  • An answer, rather than a search The first sentence answers the question. Figures sit inside the prose. Authors and dates sit on one line at the end, so you can check any of it.
  • Disagreement surfaces on the way in A contradiction is caught while the source is being filed, not months later when you act on the wrong view.
  • Nothing lands twice Every source is checked by link, then by idea. A claim you already hold comes back named as a repeat, with the earlier source.
  • Weak ground says so A brain with fewer than 10 sources opens its answers by admitting it. Ask at expert level and it grades its own evidence and names the thin spots.
  • Your assistant reads your library Add one address to Claude and it answers from what you gathered. Reading costs nothing, because no model runs on this side.
  • Cheap to feed A full source, read and filed with every position rewritten, costs under a cent.

What keeps it honest.

  • A view you replace keeps its date and moves into the evidence. Nothing is deleted.
  • Disagreements stay visible, dated on both sides, until evidence settles them.
  • An idea needs 3 separate mentions before it becomes a concept of its own.
  • Raw source text is never stored. Only the link and the reasoning survive the read.
  • Every answer names its authors and dates, so a claim can be traced to a source.

Your brains, read by the assistant you already pay for.

A brain marked public can be read from your own Claude through one address. Your assistant does the reasoning over your evidence, which is stronger than what a cheap model would write.

A brain marked private stays with you. Feeding a brain runs on your own model key, so what you spend is yours to see and cap.
